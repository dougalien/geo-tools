3D Structural Geology Explorer

Squeeze a layer cake of real southwest Montana rocks into thrusts and folds, stretch it with normal faults, add plutons, dikes and sills, then slide the land surface through it to see why a geologic map looks the way it does.

Open 3D Structural Geology Explorer (opens in a new tab)

Get started

  1. Open the app. The block starts as an undeformed layer cake.
  2. Under History, slide Compression to build thrust faults and folds, then Stretching to add normal faults. Play history runs both.
  3. Add a pluton, sill or dikes under Igneous intrusions. When each body intrudes decides what it cuts.
  4. Move the Land surface slider up and down and watch the map pattern change.
  5. Click any spot on the map or block to read what is happening there.

Tips

  • Look down shows the map view; Reset view brings the block back.
  • Topography Hard rocks stand high lifts limestone and sandstone ridges, like real erosion.
  • Reset to layer cake starts over.
  • It is a kinematic teaching model, not a mechanical simulation.
