App guide

Finite Deformable Geometry

The geometry of the universe, by measurement only. Compare supernovae, galaxy rotation, cosmic clocks, shapes and orbits as registers: each measured value divided by its ideal.

Open Finite Deformable Geometry (opens in a new tab)

Get started

  1. Start on Premise. It explains what a register is: a measured value divided by its ideal value, so the ideal sits at Q = 1.
  2. Open All registers to see every register at a glance, then Do they share a shape? to compare them on one plot.
  3. Pick any register in the sidebar for its own page: shape, flux dimming, time dilation, ageing rate, acoustic angle, cusp, rotation and more.
  4. Many pages add their own choices in the sidebar, such as which data set to use or what to plot against. Hover over points for their values.
  5. End with No fixed point, which separates what is measured from what has to be added to it.

Tips

  • Q = 1 is the ideal, which is only reached in a limit. How far a register sits from 1, and how that changes, is the information.
  • The data are published sets: Pantheon+ supernovae, SPARC galaxy rotation curves, cosmic chronometers, Planck, and crater counts on the Moon and Mars. Each page names its source.
  • If nobody has used the app for a while it goes to sleep. Click the button that says the app is asleep (“Yes, get this app back up”) and give it up to a minute.
