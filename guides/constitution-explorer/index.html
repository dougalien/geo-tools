App guide

Constitution Explorer

Ask questions in your own words and read the U.S. Constitution, landmark Supreme Court cases and the big debates, with primary sources for every answer.

Open Constitution Explorer (opens in a new tab)

Get started

  1. Ask a question in your own words, such as “Can my school search my backpack?” You get a short answer first, then the exact words of the Constitution behind it.
  2. Read the Constitution: the Preamble, all seven Articles and all 27 Amendments, each with a plain-language explanation.
  3. Supreme Court cases gives the question, the decision, the vote and why it matters for 37 landmark cases, including the student-rights cases.
  4. Big debates lays out the strongest case for both sides of ten questions people disagree about.
  5. Test yourself with the Quiz (five questions a round), or browse Fun facts.

Tips

  • Every passage links to the National Archives transcription and the Constitution Annotated on Congress.gov; every case links to the Court’s full opinion.
  • The debates don’t pick a winner. Each ends with a question to think about, which makes a good class discussion starter.
  • If nobody has used the app for a while it goes to sleep. Click the button that says the app is asleep (“Yes, get this app back up”) and give it up to a minute.
